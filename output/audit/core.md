# Core replay audit

Historical review from before the final replay audit and October 7 history squash. See the [latest audit](fresh-audit.md) and [validation record](fresh-validation.json) for the release prepared for publication.

The core now operates only on recorded sessions. RaceStore no longer owns a live connection, broadcast delay, reconnect loop, heartbeat health, or live reducer. Calendar, standings, included timing sample, and saved session library remain independent of playback.

ReplayArchive stores typed source-rate coordinate and car telemetry samples with low-rate timing records. ReplayDataset retains one flat array per high-frequency channel and per-driver index ranges. It does not create a dictionary for each animation frame. All recorded channels stay available throughout playback and arbitrary backwards seeking; there is no transient minute-window replacement. Archive preparation and source decoding occur through detached startup work or the provider actor, outside the main actor.

The render clock is separate from timing rows. RaceStore publishes rows, messages, weather, and playback time four times per second while visible playback runs. The native renderer derives a session date from ReplayClock and asks for map or selected-driver telemetry at that cursor. These queries do not mutate observable state. Continuous x/y, speed, throttle, and RPM values interpolate only between recorded observations no more than two seconds apart. Gear, brake, and DRS use the preceding observation within that valid bracket. A missing bracket or larger gap yields no car position or stale telemetry with unknown metrics. The engine does not extrapolate beyond the recorded channel or infer retirement from missing samples.

Playback supports 0.1–256× speed, explicit slow motion, restart, time nudges, lap seeking, event seeking, and up to six comparison drivers. Pause, hidden coverage, minimization, and application inactivity freeze the clock immediately while preserving play intent. A post-sleep cancellation and visibility guard prevents queued ticks from advancing hidden playback. Rate changes synchronize elapsed time at the previous rate before resetting the render anchor.

Recorded pit intervals and driver status changes reconstruct at the cursor. An explicit pit entry without a recorded exit stays active through the recording's end. Explicit retired or finished status takes precedence over the pit label while retaining the separate inPit value. Session and track status are separate. Qualifying phases preserve explicit Q1/Q2/Q3 records, including later track-status updates without phase metadata. Best-lap timing prefers dated source observations when available, including explicit empty resets at a phase transition. This keeps participating drivers' current-phase best distinct from eliminated drivers' unchanged source best. Archives without those source observations use indexed phase-specific recorded-lap histories; Race and Sprint keep an all-session derived best. Completed laps remain unavailable to the timing table until their completion date; recorded lap analysis can inspect the full archive.

A lap deletion or reinstatement is attributed only when the control message contains one driver and one matching recorded lap time, choosing the closest completed matching lap before the message. The message's lap field alone does not identify the affected lap. Dated validity changes preserve the derived best recorded lap before deletion and restore it only after reinstatement. Ambiguous messages leave validity unknown. Full-session analysis uses final recorded validity, without claiming official classification. Distance comparisons start at the actual lap boundary, use recorded cumulative distance when available and monotonic, otherwise integrate recorded speed, and reject gaps or missing coverage instead of inventing alignment. Comparisons use shared raw metres; different measured path lengths can make the endpoint delta differ from the whole-lap time difference.

Opening or downloading a session cancels the previous load, invalidates its progress callbacks, and installs only the current uncancelled result. Startup uses a disk-only completed-archive lookup and rechecks cancellation after awaiting it. A complete downloaded Monaco archive supersedes the included sample. The explicit download action bypasses the sample shortcut and upgrades migrated timing-only archives. Explicit refresh preserves the committed archive while the provider verifies its replacement. Cancelled downloads remain discoverable for resumption. Session, calendar, and standings errors remain separate.

Final integrated verification passed 73 tests in eight suites with strict Swift 6, complete concurrency checking, and warnings as errors. Thirteen replay-engine regressions cover interpolation boundaries and gaps, continuous versus discrete telemetry, malformed metrics, explicit pit and retirement state across backwards seeks, qualifying phase and status isolation, lap-start distance alignment and gap rejection, render clock freezing, chronological lap deletion and reinstatement, rejection of ambiguous validity messages, open pit entries, terminal status precedence, source best-lap resets, old/new archive reloads, and phase-specific derived best laps. Existing chronological scan equivalence, JSON decoding, lap completion, track geometry, and cancellation tests remain. Additional lifecycle regressions cover replay controls, bounded comparisons, slow motion, continuous rate changes, and the queued ticker. [Replay overhaul validation](replay-overhaul.md) records release, native, and real archive checks.

The earlier replay benchmark measured 5,000 equivalent random lookups at 42.554 seconds for the chronological scan and 0.220 seconds for indexed snapshots, about 193 times faster for that operation. The earlier matched decoder benchmark measured five archives at 5.028 seconds with Codable and 2.245 seconds with Foundation. Those measurements were taken before the replay overhaul, in Swift 5 language mode, and remain labelled historical in performance.json. They are not a whole-app speed claim. The optimized source-rate fixture contains 576,020 coordinate samples and 576,020 telemetry samples over two hours. On held sources before the final pit and qualifying-best corrections, dataset preparation took 0.139917 seconds; 10,000 map-plus-selected-driver queries took 0.149022 seconds, about 14.9 microseconds per query, with checksum 2440000. Ten thousand full timing snapshots took 0.084411 seconds with checksum 200000. The fixture has no pit intervals or qualifying phases. Integrated tests compiled during this run; absolute durations vary with machine load. This excludes native drawing and transport. The source-rate benchmark is reproduced with `bash scripts/benchmark-core.sh --recorded-only`; the sparse bundled timing benchmark remains available without that argument. Both source-rate measurements are preserved in performance.json.

No process RSS, energy use, or transport latency was measured by the core harness. Typed sample strides and counts describe retained records, not exact process memory. Provider-owned storage limits and chunk checks apply to downloads. Actual source coverage determines available replay channels and lap comparisons; the included sample has partial channels. Production archives require source and native validation beyond synthetic regression fixtures.
